Yellow ochre

100,000 years ago

The oldest colour people used. A paint workshop about 100,000 years old, with ochre ground in abalone shells, was found in Blombos Cave, South Africa.

Material
Iron-rich clay
Origin
Roussillon, France
History
At least 100,000 years ago
Chemistry
Goethite, an iron oxyhydroxide, in clay
Formula
FeO(OH)·nH2O

Egyptian blue

4,500 years ago

The first colour anyone made rather than found. Under infrared light it glows, which is how conservators find its traces on ancient statues.

Material
Sand, copper and lime, fired together
Origin
Egypt
History
More than 4,500 years ago
Chemistry
Calcium copper silicate
Formula
CaCuSi4O10

£38 per 20 g jar

Tyrian purple

3,500 years ago

Thousands of snails give a single gram. In late imperial Rome, the law reserved the finest purple for the emperor.

Material
Glands of murex sea snails
Origin
Tyre, on the coast of Lebanon
History
Phoenician, from about 1500 BCE
Chemistry
6,6′-dibromoindigo
Formula
C16H8Br2N2O2

Two grams a year

£2,600 per 1 g jar

Ultramarine

1,500 years ago

Its name means “beyond the sea”. In Renaissance Italy the best grade could cost more than gold, so contracts stated how much of it a painter had to use.

Material
Lapis lazuli
Origin
Sar-e-Sang, Badakhshan, Afghanistan
History
Afghan cave paintings, 6th century; Renaissance Italy
Chemistry
Lazurite, a sodium aluminium silicate holding sulfur
Formula
(Na,Ca)8(AlSiO4)6(S,SO4,Cl)2

£180 per 5 g jar

Carmine

500 years ago

About 70,000 dried insects make a pound of dye. Under Spanish rule it became one of Mexico’s most valuable exports, second only to silver.

Material
Cochineal insects from prickly pear cactus
Origin
Oaxaca, Mexico
History
Aztec and Maya; in Europe from the 16th century
Chemistry
Carminic acid
Formula
C22H20O13

£34 per 10 g jar

Bone black

Today

Burn bone without air and what is left is a warm, deep black. The finest version, ivory black, is no longer made.

Material
Bone, charred without air
Origin
Everywhere
History
Prehistoric to today
Chemistry
Carbon and calcium phosphate
Formula
C + Ca3(PO4)2

£12 per 50 g jar
