Yellow ochre

100,000 years ago

Ochre, yellow and red, is the oldest colour people used. In Blombos Cave, South Africa, people mixed it and stored it in abalone shells about 100,000 years ago.

Material
Iron-rich clay
Origin
Roussillon, France
History
At least 100,000 years ago
Chemistry
Goethite, an iron oxyhydroxide, in clay
Formula
FeO(OH)·nH2O

Bone black

30,000 years ago, and still made today

Burn bone without air and what is left is a warm, deep black. The finest version, ivory black, is no longer made.

Material
Bone, charred without air
Origin
Everywhere
History
Prehistoric to today
Chemistry
Carbon and calcium phosphate
Formula
C + Ca3(PO4)2

Egyptian blue

4,500 years ago

The first colour anyone made rather than found. Lit by ordinary light, it gives off infrared that a camera can see, which is how conservators find its traces on ancient statues.

Material
Sand, copper and lime, fired together
Origin
Egypt
History
More than 4,500 years ago
Chemistry
Calcium copper silicate
Formula
CaCuSi4O10

£38 per 20 g jar

Ultramarine

1,500 years ago

Its name means “beyond the sea”. In Renaissance Italy the best grade could cost more than gold, so contracts stated how much of it a painter had to use.

Grind it yourself
Material
Lapis lazuli
Origin
Sar-e-Sang, Badakhshan, Afghanistan
History
Afghan cave paintings, 6th century; Renaissance Italy
Chemistry
Lazurite, a sodium aluminium silicate holding sulfur
Formula
(Na,Ca)8(AlSiO4)6(S,SO4,Cl)2

£180 per 5 g jar

Carmine

500 years in Europe
