Where colour comes from

For centuries before paint came in tubes, a painter bought colour as a lump of stone, a jar of dried insects or a cake of leaf dye, and ground it by hand.

The collapsible metal paint tube was patented in 1841.

Every pigment we keep still comes from one of eight sources.

Point at a specimen, or tap it, and its colour goes into the water.

  • Iron-rich clay.

  • Sand, copper and lime, fired together.

  • Lapis lazuli.

  • Glands of murex sea snails.

  • Lead, vinegar and heat. Toxic, so archive only.

  • Cochineal insects from prickly pear cactus.

  • Leaves of Indigofera tinctoria.

  • Bone, charred without air.
