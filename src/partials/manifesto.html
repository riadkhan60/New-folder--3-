Why we go down

At 200 metres, about one percent of the sunlight is left. Below 1,000 metres, the only light is the light living things make. Roughly three quarters of the sea floor has never been mapped in detail. Its deepest point, the Challenger Deep, is 10,935 metres down: deeper than Everest is tall, by two kilometres. Fewer people have been there than have been to space.

Noctiluca scintillans, the plankton we are named after, flashes blue when the water around it is disturbed.
